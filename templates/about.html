The long way into data

My path started with a mechanical engineering degree from IIT Ropar in India, followed by two years in automotive manufacturing at Tata Motors.

I completed a master's in aerospace engineering at the University of Toronto in 2020 — right as COVID hit and the engineering job market went quiet. I took on whatever work I could find, from truck mechanic to Uber deliveries, while I figured out my next move. A friend working as a Registered Energy Advisor introduced me to the field, and I was hooked.

I got certified and spent the next several years walking through hundreds of homes across Canada with a blower door, a thermal camera, and a methodology built on decades of building science research. Every audit fed into a report and an energy model: air leakage measurements, heat loss coefficients, equipment efficiency ratings, estimated savings by upgrade path. I was doing data work long before I called it that.

Now I'm making that transition explicit — learning Python, SQL, Power BI, and statistical analysis, and working toward a Data Analyst or Energy Data Analyst role where I can combine that field experience with analytical rigour.

My edge isn't just technical skill. It's knowing what the numbers mean before the model runs, and knowing which questions are worth asking in the first place.
